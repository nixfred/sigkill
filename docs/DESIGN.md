# SIGKILL design doc

Native first-person shooter for Omarchy (Arch + Hyprland). You are the nixfred >_ hexagon hunting rogue daemons, zombie processes and runaway apps in a terminal arena.

## Decided
- Name: SIGKILL (Fred).
- Native installable Linux game, not web.
- Code lives at the dev machine; Larry (herdr workspace "sigkill") writes all code.
- Safety: game never modifies, deletes, executes, signals or kills anything real. Filesystem/process features read names, sizes, counts only. Demo-data mode for anything public. No Ctrl+Alt hotkeys, no focus stealing, no GPU use when not running.
- Brand: locked nixfred hexagon (icon-only, white >_) on icon, repo, README.

- Visual direction: Tron light-grid plus Matrix code rain (Fred).
- Easy internet multiplayer (room codes/invite links) is a core feature (Fred).
- Signature feature: the player's own real local AI is their in-game guide, shown live as theirs (Fred).
- Quality bar: Rockstar-level polish; Larry builds in small numbered chunks, each verified before the next (Fred).
- Full spec: PRD.md (docs/PRD.md in repo).

- (2026-10-25, Fred) Player AI = the Omarchy default agent (~/.config/omarchy/defaults/agent), called directly in non-interactive mode with tools off, never via omarchy-agent. Fallback: local model server, then scripted guide. Live GPU stats on boot and HUD. Safety rule reworded to allow only this one execution. New chunk C9b (agent adapters).
- Enemy art bar (Fred, 2026-10-25 10:23 PM ET): no cartoon monsters. Daemons are faceless, geometric, menacing: black obsidian shards with floating red terminal glyphs, angular red drone swarms, and a giant red-hot lattice core as the CPU boss. Kills derezz into glowing voxel cubes. Reference: readme-art/sigkill-epic-1.jpg and sigkill-epic-2.jpg (concept art).
- Arsenal (Fred, 2026-10-25 10:25 PM ET): many weapons, each a signal or shell command made physical (kill -9 rail rifle, fork() shotgun, grep seeker, rm -rf cannon, cron mines, ping sonar, chmod 000 freeze ray, pipe chain beam, /dev/null black hole, sudo overcharge, plus the signal set), with mods, light skins and unlocks. Full list in PRD 5.2.
- Story (Fred, 2026-10-25 10:25 PM ET): cyberpunk + Matrix + Tron campaign. A sysadmin jacked into their own machine hunts processes they never started; their AI is the only one they trust, until the trail asks who wrote the daemons. PRD 5.6, milestone M6.
- Opener (Fred, 2026-10-25 10:45 PM ET): 2-minute trapped-in-the-game cold open built from the player's real machine (fake crash in their theme, real usernames/folders/uptime, a copy of them as the first daemon, "SIGKILL sent by you to you"). Real data read-only and shown live only; Esc always works; opt-in with fake-data fallback. PRD 5.7, chunks C38-C42.
- Community prompts (Fred, 2026-10-26): build-in-public on X; accepted prompts get credited. First: Albert / @QuantaCortex's Audit Trail visor (PRD 5.5, C43). Credits roll is a live top-style process table with contributors as processes, Fred as PID 1, ending in a kill -9 derezz (PRD 5.8, C44).
- SIGSTOP fairness (producer, C18, 2026-10-27): in a match SIGSTOP never slows the host's simulation, or anyone's; nothing in a match touches the engine's time scale. The effect is self-only and local: the look and the pitched-down sound on that player's own screen, and zero spread and zero recoil only while the host says it is on. The host owns and validates every SIGSTOP meter and drops (and counts) a start the meter cannot pay for. Only solo does the world slow (0.3x), with the player compensated to 0.9x.
- Relay transport (producer, C16d, 2026-10-28): WebSocket mode added for proxy-only hosts (exe.dev); UDP mode stays as the VPS alternative; the game picks transport from the relay URL scheme.
- Fork bomb, no self-damage (producer, C19, 2026-10-28): a thrower is never hurt by its own fork bomb's blasts, solo or in a match; it keeps it fun.
- Fork bomb, host-owned chain (producer, C19, 2026-10-28): in a match the host owns the whole chain. A client sends only its throw (origin and aim), which the host validates against its view of the player's position and aim and its count of the bombs; the host simulates every bomblet and judges all damage; clients get one seed per throw to draw the same scatter, and the host's blast events, and never decide damage.
- Browser version (Fred, 2026-10-28 12:46 AM ET): parked for release 2.0, not 1.0. Plan: a Godot web export (WebGL renderer, shader fallbacks) using the WebSocket relay for multiplayer, demo-data maps only (no filesystem, processes, local announcer or voice in a browser). Native game ships first.
- Light-trail mode (producer, 2026-10-28): an optional match mode, off by default, turned on by the host in the Esc menu's settings (Multiplayer, "light trails") or with the launch flag `-- --light-trails`; the host's setting is the match's and joiners get it from the host. Every player's movement on the ground lays thin neon wall segments behind them in that player's own colour from the theme palette (no new art assets: simple meshes and materials); airborne lays none. Segments live a few seconds, then fade and free, with a per-player cap. Touching any light wall, anyone's, your own older trail included, kills you with the existing derezz death and credits the trail's owner (a self-kill if your own); your own newest segments get a short grace. A death or respawn clears that player's trail; leaving the match frees all of it. In a match the host is authoritative for trail kills, in the netcode's existing pattern; joiners see the same walls; the relay protocol is unchanged. Numbers (resources/light_trail.tres): a segment every 0.5 m of ground travel, 1.2 m tall, 0.08 m thick, standing 6 s with a 0.75 s fade, 64 segments a player, 0.4 s of grace on your own (your newest never touches you), the ground within 0.15 m of the feet, a new wall past a 2.5 m gap.
- Code-rain vision (producer, 2026-10-28): an ability, not a weapon. Hold V (a new rebindable action, "code_rain_vision", in the settings' key list; never a Ctrl+Alt binding; keyboard and mouse only for now); releasing it or an empty meter turns it off. Its own meter, separate from SIGSTOP's: a full meter lasts about 3 s of use, refills fully in about 12 s after about 1 s of rest, and needs at least about 25% to start; every number in resources/code_rain_vision.tres. While on, every enemy body within about 40 m (the target dummies alone; the other players in a match) is drawn as a green glyph silhouette that shows through walls, built from the existing code rain look (no new art assets, simple materials and shaders only), and the world dims and tints toward green on your screen only; your weapons fire as usual. Purely local and visual: in a match it draws only positions the client already has; no new network messages, protocol stays 6, the host's authority and the relay untouched, and nothing about your vision is sent to anyone. It respects the comfort settings (a calmer tint, no flashing), and the HUD shows its meter next to SIGSTOP's. A death or respawn turns it off and leaves the meter as it was; leaving a match turns it off. Numbers picked: meter 3 s, refill 12 s after 1 s of rest, start 25%, range 40 m, tint (0.45, 0.8, 0.5) at strength 1 or 0.5 calm, 0.2 s ease, silhouettes 0.6 m by 1.9 m with glow 3, the nearest 16 at most.
- Root vs user and sudo overcharge (producer, 2026-10-28): every life starts as user; the HUD shows a small prompt badge, "user$" normally and "root#" while overcharged, in the theme's warning red for root. About 3 kills in one life without dying (target dummy derezzes alone; kills credited to you in a match, light-trail wall kills included) earn one sudo charge; at most 1 held; dying resets the streak and drops an unused charge (you respawn as user). Press Z (a new rebindable action, "sudo_overcharge", in the settings key list; never a Ctrl+Alt binding): the HUD flashes a purely cosmetic "[sudo] password for user: ********" line, then root# for about 8 s. While root the weapon in hand (and any you swap to) does about 1.5x damage and uses no ammo; the rail's ult meter is not filled faster and its shot is not doubled (it just gets the 1.5x); a clear overcharge look on the weapon and muzzle (simple tint and glow, no new art); SIGSTOP and code-rain vision unchanged. Root ends at the timer, on death, on respawn and on leaving a match. All numbers (kills needed 3, duration 8 s, damage multiplier 1.5, charges held at most 1) in resources/sudo_overcharge.tres. Alone all local; in a match host-owned: the host counts kills, grants the charge, accepts or refuses activation, applies the 1.5x in its own shot judging and tells clients who is root so everyone sees the badge and glow; the minimum new messages, protocol 7, an old-protocol peer gets the existing version-mismatch refusal; clients never apply the multiplier themselves; the relay untouched beyond carrying the new messages. Fiction only: the game never runs, calls or mentions a real sudo command or reads anything about the real user; "user" is a fixed word, not the player's username.
- Spectate as top (producer, 2026-10-28): while you are dead in a match (from your derezz until your respawn, the existing 3 s respawn delay) your HUD shows a full-width top-style table of the match, drawn in the Omarchy theme colours, monospace, readable at 960x540; it goes away the moment you respawn, and the dead player's camera stays as it is (this chunk is the table, not a new camera). Hold Tab (a new rebindable action, "show_top", in the settings key list; never a Ctrl+Alt binding) to show the same table any time, alive or dead, alone or in a match; alone the rows are you plus the target dummies as daemon processes (COMMAND "dummy-1" and on, state Z while derezzing or respawning). Header, top-style: "top - <round clock> up <time since match start>, <N> users, load average: a, b, c", the load being the match's kills in the last 10, 30 and 60 seconds of play, then "Tasks: <total> total, <r> running, <s> stopped, <z> zombie". Columns: PID (a stable game number per player for the match, by join order from 100; game data, not a real PID), USER ("user" or "root" from C22's root state; fixed words, never the real username), S (R alive, Z dead, T while that player's SIGSTOP is on if the viewer's game knows it, else R), %CPU (that player's share of the damage dealt this round, 0.0 to 100.0), RES (health), TIME+ (time alive this life, m:ss.hh), K (kills), D (deaths), COMMAND (the player's in-game name); sorted by K descending, then %CPU descending, like top; your own row highlighted. It refreshes once a second, like top, not every frame. All numbers (the 1.0 s refresh, the 10/30/60 load windows, the rows shown at most, PID base 100) in resources/spectate_top.tres. Network: built from what every peer already receives where it can; only a column that truly needs host data gets one minimal host-owned table message at no more than 1 Hz, protocol 8, an old-protocol peer getting the existing version-mismatch refusal (with no message the protocol stays 7); the host's authority and the relay untouched beyond carrying it; a client never invents another player's numbers. During the round break the summary has priority: the table hides or keeps clear of it, so the two never overlap at 960x540. Fiction only: the table never reads, lists or mentions real processes, PIDs, users, hostnames or paths; no OS.get_process_id, /proc, ps, top, OS.execute or OS.create_process in the new code.
- fork() shotgun (producer, 2026-10-28): weapon slot 5 (key 5, and in the Q last-weapon swap and the weapon cycle like the others). Each shot fires a spread of pellets that are short-lived fast projectiles, not instant hitscan, so the split is real and visible: each parent pellet travels a fork distance and is then replaced by two child pellets angled apart by a fork angle in the plane of the spread, flying on at the same speed; one fork generation normally (6 pellets become 12). A parent that hits something before its fork distance does full pellet damage and never forks; each child does a fraction of its parent's damage (0.6 to start), so point-blank does the most total damage and range spreads it out. Pellets have a longest range and then vanish, and they stop at walls. sudo overcharge (C22) on this weapon: the children fork once more (two generations, up to 24), using the overcharge rules already in place, and no other overcharge effect. Fire rate, magazine and E reload like the other shotguns, slower than SIGTERM; its own light colour from the theme palette with thin glowing pellet trails, and a fork shows as a small flash; the kill feed and the callouts follow the existing pattern ("fork()"). Every number (pellet count, speed, fork distance, fork angle, child damage fraction, generations, overcharge generations, the longest range, fire interval, magazine, reload time, spread) in resources/fork_shotgun.tres, in the existing weapon tuning style, no magic numbers in code. Network: the host stays authoritative for hits and damage like the other weapons, reusing the existing shot and hit messages so the protocol stays 8 if it can (a truly needed new message would mean protocol 9, old peers refused with the existing version mismatch); the pellet simulation is deterministic from the shot's origin, direction and seed, so every peer draws the same forks, and a client never decides damage; the relay untouched beyond carrying traffic. Fiction only: the weapon never calls fork(), OS.execute or OS.create_process, reads /proc or touches anything real.
- grep seeker (producer, 2026-10-28): weapon slot 6 (key 6, and in the Q last-weapon swap and the weapon cycle like the others), PRD 5.2's seeker smart gun on the C16 framework. Marking: while grep is in hand and the existing aim action is held (or toggled, per the C16b setting), a target (a dummy alone, another player in a match) inside a narrow lock cone round the crosshair, within lock range and with line of sight, builds a lock over a lock time and is then marked; up to a cap of marks at once (3). A mark drops when its target leaves line of sight or range for longer than a grace time, derezzes, or the weapon is swapped. Marked targets show a thin bracket in grep's own theme colour with a small "match" tag, the brackets tightening as a lock builds; the HUD shows "grep -c: N". No new key bindings. Firing: one seeker dart per mark (spread over the marks), or one straight dart with no mark; darts are fast projectiles with a limited turn rate (they home, and can miss a target that breaks hard or ducks behind cover), stop at walls, vanish at their range; a shot spends the marks; damage per dart, fire interval, magazine and E reload like the other weapons, mid-range power, weaker a hit than the cannon, better at tracking. sudo overcharge (C22): its general rules hold (1.5 times the damage, no ammo spent, host-judged), plus grep's own: locks build twice as fast and the cap on marks rises to 5; nothing else. Every number in resources/grep_seeker.tres, no magic numbers in code. Look: its own light colour (not the cannon's, SIGTERM's, the rail's, the fork bomb's or fork()'s), thin glowing dart trails; kill feed and callouts "grep". Network: the host stays authoritative; the shot intent carries the shooter's claimed mark target ids, the host re-checks each claim (range, line of sight, cone with a small tolerance, target alive) and drops bad ones, simulates the darts deterministically from origin, aim, seed and the believed targets and judges them with the existing combat path (root included); existing messages reused, protocol 8 if at all possible, a truly needed change means protocol 9 and the existing version refusal; joiners draw the same darts and never decide damage; the relay untouched. Fiction only: nothing runs grep, OS.execute or OS.create_process, reads /proc, files or anything real. The strip must fit six slots at 960x540. As built, three readings of ours; the producer confirmed the first and the third after the build (2026-10-28), the second stands as ours: "line of sight" for keeping a mark means in sight, within a sight cone of the crosshair (40 degrees, a target behind you is out of sight) with nothing of the level between, and the host's "cone with a small tolerance" is that sight cone plus 4 degrees (the narrow 6 degree lock cone at the shot would refuse any mark but the one under the crosshair); the cannon has no headshot bonus, so grep's 18 a dart is set under the cannon's 25 a hit; and protocol 9, since the intent must carry the claims and the seed message the believed marks, two existing messages gaining a field (no new message), and a protocol 8 peer still connecting would stall a joiner's ammo count on its first grep shot.
- rm -rf cannon (producer, 2026-10-28): weapon slot 7 (key 7, and in the Q last-weapon swap and the weapon cycle like the others), PRD 5.2's slow, devastating cannon with friendly-fire risk, on the C16 framework. Confirm wind-up: fire starts a short wind-up (the "are you sure" beat) before the shot leaves, with a small "rm: remove? [y]" prompt on the HUD filling as it charges; letting go early does not cancel it, the shot goes when it ends; a swap, a death or a respawn during it cancels it and spends no ammo; while it winds up and just after the shot the shooter moves slower (a move speed factor). No new key bindings. Shot: one slow, heavy glowing slug flying straight, clearly slower than every other projectile, detonating on a wall, the floor, any target or at its longest range. Blast: area damage falling off from the centre to the edge of its radius, enough at the centre to derezz a full-health player and only a scratch at the edge; level geometry between blast and target blocks it. Friendly fire: the blast hurts everything in its radius, the shooter included at a tuned fraction (point-blank is a real mistake); any team or co-op grouping is ignored, and with none yet it is simply everyone; a self-kill reads as the shooter removing themself ("rm -rf: <name> removed themself"), other kills "rm -rf". Ammo: magazine 1, a small reserve, a long E reload, a long fire interval. sudo (C22): its general rules (1.5x damage, no ammo spent, host-judged) plus "--no-preserve-root": a larger blast radius and a shorter wind-up; self-damage still applies; nothing else. Every number in resources/rm_rf_cannon.tres. Look: its own palette colour, a heavy glowing slug with a thick trail, a bright expanding blast ring; feed and callouts "rm -rf". Network: the host stays authoritative; the usual intent (sent when the wind-up ends); the host runs the slug and blast and judges every damage and kill with the existing combat path (root and self-damage included); joiners draw them and never decide damage; existing messages, protocol 9 if possible; the relay untouched. Fiction only, a hard rule: nothing runs rm, OS.execute, OS.create_process, DirAccess.remove, OS.move_to_trash or touches files, /proc or the system, with a test that greps for them. Seven slots at 960x540 clear of the guide panel. Ours as built (the brief's "decide toward slow, devastating, readable, fair"): ember orange (#ff6a1a) as its colour, between hostile amber and red but neither, the blast's danger in it; the wind-up is the rail's charge (so the Esc menu cancels it too, as it does the rail's) and its prompt types out on the strip's third line, where the rail's charge shows; no slide starts while the weight is on; alone the player has no health (as since C14), so the blast only finds the daemons and the self-damage is a match's; each blast of the shooter's is called out "rm -rf" at once, as the fork bomb's first blast is; a blast on a surface goes off 5 cm back from it, so the surface shields what is behind; a joiner's slug passes bodies, holds at a wall or its range and goes off only on the host's word (fork bomb's _blast), waiting 0.5 s for it; the slot line at the small lines' 12 px; the numbers (0.6 s and 0.3 s, half pace for 0.8 s, 9 m/s, 45 m, 6 m and 9 m, 140 to 8, half to the shooter, 1 round, 3 in reserve, 3.2 s, 2.5 s).
